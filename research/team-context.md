## 2. HISTORICAL PATTERNS (with evidence + source)

1. **RB Dead Zone (Rounds 3-6):** RBs taken in this range have historically underperformed ADP relative to WRs in the same range, because RB scoring flattens from rounds 3/4 through the single digits while WR scoring stays elevated through rounds 5-6. — Establish The Run, "The Death of the Running Back Dead Zone" and "Reconsidering the RB Dead Zone in a Changing Ecosystem" (establishtherun.com). **Rule:** Prefer WR over RB in rounds 3-6 unless the RB has a clear standalone workhorse role; fill RB via ambiguous-backfield/handcuff discounts instead.

2. **Ambiguous-backfield ADP discount:** RBs who fall in ADP due to committee/role uncertainty (rookies, low draft capital, timeshares) are systematically undervalued relative to their per-touch efficiency — the market overweights "clarity" and underweights talent. — Establish The Run / FantasyLife "RB Dead Zone Strategy 2026" (fantasylife.com, 2026). **Rule:** Actively target backs in murky committees rather than avoiding them; that "murkiness" is priced in as a discount, not a warning.

3. **RB reliability vs. WR reliability is overstated as a WR-safety argument:** Top-12 ADP RBs have been about as reliable as top-12 ADP WRs historically — the "RBs are too risky" heuristic used to justify pure zero-RB is not fully supported. — CBS Sports positional-reliability analysis (referenced via CBS Sports draft-prep coverage). **Rule:** Don't treat round-1/2 RB picks as inherently riskier than round-1/2 WR picks; injury variance is the real driver, not positional volatility per se.

4. **Elite-TE positional advantage persists:** Because rostered TE talent is scarce, an elite top-3/4 TE creates a weekly points-over-replacement edge no other position matches at that draft cost; only the top tier gains real value even in TE-premium scoring. — JJ Zachariason (via FantasyPros consensus-rankings context) and Yahoo "2026 Fantasy Football TE Preview" (2026). **Rule:** If a top-4 TE falls to a value price, take it — mid-tier TEs do not carry the same edge and are replaceable off waivers.

5. **Late-round QB is viable, not clearly optimal, and rushing floor is the differentiator:** In recent seasons, 4 of the QB21-32 range finished top-15 at the position; the reliable "hit" profile skews toward QBs with a real rushing floor (~30+ rush yds/gm threshold). Paying up for a 2nd startable QB is generally a worse use of a pick than a 2nd shot at WR/RB. — FantasyPros "Late-Round Quarterback Fantasy Football Draft Strategy & Targets (2026)" (fantasypros.com, 2026). **Rule:** Punt QB unless a rushing-floor QB free-falls; never draft a true QB2 as "insurance" — stream instead.

6. **Rookie WR breakouts are increasingly common but still a minority outcome; Year 2-3 is the modal breakout window.** A rookie WR has finished top-6 at the position in 4 of the last 5 seasons, but historically 52% of first-time WR1 breakouts arrive in Year 2 or 3, and 68% have arrived by Year 3. Only ~15% of rookie WRs hit top-48 (startable) in year one, concentrated among first-round draft capital (56% hit rate for 1st-rounders). — FantasyPros "Identifying the Next Breakout Wide Receivers (2026)" and Footballguys "Draft Capital Matters: Rookie Wide Receivers" (2026). **Rule:** Draft-capital-plus-target-competition matters more than "rookie WR" as a label; prioritize Year-2/3 breakout candidates in a strong new offensive environment over unproven rookies at the same ADP.

7. **Preseason hype is mostly noise; preseason touchdowns are the single most misleading signal.** Players who spike 2+ rounds in ADP off preseason performance are being priced on small samples against backups/scheme-vanilla defenses. — "The Preseason Hype Trap: What August Actually Tells You," Fantasy Football Blueprint (Aug 2, 2026). **Rule:** Fade players whose ADP rose sharply purely off preseason box scores (e.g., De'Zhaun Stribling-type 2026 risers cited in the same coverage) unless role/target-share data corroborates it; instead buy the "August discount" on proven players whose ADP quietly slipped.

8. **Positional scarcity math still favors elite-tier concentration, not broad hoarding.** Scarcity only matters at the top of a position's distribution — hoarding mid-tier players at a "scarce" position doesn't replicate the elite-tier edge. — Fantasy Football Almanac, "Fantasy Football Positional Scarcity Explained" (2026-03-20). **Rule:** Apply scarcity logic only to top-4 TE / true bellcow RB tiers; don't overpay mid-tier players at a position just because the position "feels thin."
